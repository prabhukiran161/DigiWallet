DigiWall Project Requirements
Functional scope and implementation specification
Version 1.0  |  11 November 2026  |  Product and engineering baseline
Purpose and central decision
DigiWall is a personal finance tracker that brings bank balances, physical cash, credit cards, investments, borrowed money, lent money, transactions and budgets into one wallet. It helps users understand where their money is held, how it is spent, what remains available and what they owe or are owed.
Loan proceeds are actual money in a bank or cash account. A loan is a liability, and a funding allocation is a label that reserves and traces some of that money for a purpose. Spending loan proceeds reduces the payment account once and consumes the allocation; it does not repay the loan. Repayment separately reduces cash and loan principal. This distinction governs the entire specification.
Requirement authority
Confirmed requirements are the user decisions consolidated in Section 2. The allocation model is the recommended resolution requested for loan accounting. Other implementation defaults are explicitly identified as recommendations in Section 15. “Shall” denotes a testable requirement for the proposed V1 baseline; recommendations are not represented as previously confirmed decisions.

1 Scope and financial language
V1 product boundary
The recommended V1 is a manual tracking application. Users record financial events; DigiWall does not hold money, connect to banks, issue cards or execute payments. Records describe real-world activity and can be corrected with an audit trail. The application supports multiple users with strictly private financial data and a shared institution catalog.
Term
Required meaning
Wallet available balance
Sum of recorded bank and physical cash balances. Excludes investments, receivables, credit limits and loan liabilities. Includes borrowed cash already received.
Unallocated cash
Available balance minus active cash reservations. A separate planning metric, never a replacement for the confirmed wallet balance.
Loan principal outstanding
Principal received or brought forward, less principal repaid or explicitly forgiven. It is not unused loan proceeds.
Remaining loan allocation
Received proceeds still reserved for a purpose, after funded use and releases. It is neither extra cash nor a liability balance.
Receivable
Principal lent to another party and still recoverable. An asset excluded from available cash.
Investment carrying value
Recorded opening value plus contributions less return of contributed capital and recorded losses. V1 does not imply current market value.
Credit and debit in the UI
User-friendly money-in and money-out actions. The backend uses explicit event types; formal ledger debit and credit have their accounting meanings.
Recorded net worth
Recorded assets minus recorded liabilities. Contributions-only investment values make this a book-value overview, not a market valuation.

Out of scope for the initial release
Live bank synchronization, payment execution, trading, market-price feeds, automated interest accrual, EMI amortization, tax calculations, multi-currency conversion and shared household wallets are deferred. Automatic schedules and reminders may be added later; a SIP in V1 is an investment label with manually entered contributions.

3 Wallet accounts and institutions
FR01  The dashboard shall show available balance, monthly liquid inflow and outflow, expense total, budget remaining, recorded net worth and a separately labeled reserved or unallocated cash summary. Each metric shall expose its definition.
FR02  Account cards shall show names, institution logos or fallbacks, account type, current balance and monthly account inflows and outflows. Credit cards shall instead emphasize debt, limit and remaining limit; loans shall distinguish debt from funding remaining.
FR03  Users shall create, edit and archive bank, physical cash, credit card, investment, loan and lending records. Names may be customized. Existing records with transactions shall not be hard-deleted. Archiving shall not remove balances from historical or current totals.
FR04  Account setup shall allow zero or a dated opening balance. Credit card setup shall capture a limit and opening debt separately; existing loans and receivables shall capture opening principal without creating new cash. An optional debit card is metadata linked to one bank account.
FR05  Institution search shall return catalog names and logos. Custom submission shall collect name, institution type and an optional image, then make the validated institution available to all users. Only institution metadata is shared; user accounts, balances and identifiers remain private.
FR06  Catalog creation shall normalize Unicode, trim and collapse whitespace, and case-fold names before applying a unique key scoped by institution type and country. Aliases shall resolve common alternate names. Punctuation variants and approximate matches shall prompt review rather than silently merging unrelated institutions. Database uniqueness shall prevent concurrent duplicate creation.
FR07  Logo uploads shall validate file type, size and image dimensions, remove metadata and reject active content. Use generic icons when images are absent. A catalog steward shall be able to correct names, logos and aliases without changing private account ownership.
Account setup and lifecycle rules
Require account name, type, currency and effective opening date; require institution where applicable, with a custom institution fallback. Do not require full bank or card numbers; a user label or optional masked suffix is sufficient. An FD institution describes where the investment is held and does not imply a new bank account.
A normal transaction cannot predate the account opening date. To migrate older history, the user must first adjust the opening boundary and reconcile it. A loan or receivable can be marked settled only when its posted principal and posted interest balances are zero. Unused funding reservations must also be resolved before closing a loan.

4 Transaction workflows
FR08  A shared Credit or Debit entry screen shall first determine the business event, then show valid accounts and fields. Require positive amount, description, effective date and time and an appropriate category; notes are optional. The form shall preview every affected balance before confirmation.
FR09  Credit to a bank or cash account shall distinguish income, borrowed money, repayment received, transfer received and refund. Only income and received interest are income. For a new loan, collect lender, loan type, purpose and destination bank; create the loan and disbursement atomically.
FR10  Credit to a credit card shall mean repayment from a selected bank account, except a separately identified merchant refund. Credit to an investment shall mean contribution from a selected bank account. These events shall generate linked account views from one underlying transaction.
FR11  Debit shall distinguish expense, transfer, lending, loan repayment and investment contribution. For an expense, choose the real payment account. A loan may be selected as “Funding source” or “Purpose allocation” only, never as the cash payment account. Opening the form from a loan card may prefill that tag but must still require a payment account.
FR12  Bank-to-bank and bank-to-cash transfers shall require different source and destination accounts in the same currency. Card payments and investment contributions shall retain their confirmed bank-source restriction in V1. Fees shall be separate expense components, not hidden in transferred principal.
FR13  Lending shall record borrower, date, principal, optional due date, optional agreed interest and notes. The source bank or cash balance shall decrease while a receivable increases. Receipts shall split principal and interest; partial receipts and overdue status shall be supported.
FR14  Loan repayment shall select an existing loan and actual bank or cash source, with explicit principal, interest and fee components. The total shall equal their sum. Principal reduces debt, interest and fees are expenses, and repayment does not automatically change purpose spending.
FR15  Transactions shall support search and filters by period, account, category, event type, counterparty, loan and purpose. Show linked entries, notes and account balance after each event. Corrections shall reverse the original event and post a replacement, preserving both in the audit history.
Validation and feedback
The client shall show field-level errors and retain input after a failed save. The server shall enforce identical financial rules. Save once per user intent; duplicate submission or a network retry must not create a second financial event. Balance updates shall appear only after the complete transaction commits.

5 Loan funding allocations
Recommended model
Maintain three separate dimensions: a cash account answers where money is held; a funding allocation answers which purpose it is reserved or used for; a loan liability answers how much is owed. An allocation is a memorandum record, not another asset or another cash account. Currency is fungible; tagging records user intent and does not prove the provenance of particular banknotes.
LF01  Each disbursement shall link a loan, receiving bank and amount. Total assigned funding shall not exceed actually received proceeds. Undisbursed approved loan limits may be recorded as information but shall not increase available cash or funding capacity.
LF02  A purpose allocation shall record loan, purpose, assigned amount and the bank or cash accounts holding its remaining reservation. Users may split one loan across purposes or fund one transaction from multiple loans plus their own money. Funding splits shall never exceed the eligible transaction amount.
LF03  A cash expense shall reduce the selected payment account once and increase its expense category. Any linked funding usage shall reduce the reservation by the tagged amount, without another cash posting and without reducing loan principal.
LF04  Track funded expenses, funded asset acquisitions, principal returned from unused proceeds and released reservations separately. Funding an investment or receivable consumes an allocation but remains an asset transfer, not a budget expense. Total funding use therefore need not equal budget spending.
LF05  Moving reserved money between banks shall move its reservation explicitly with the linked transfer. It shall not count as funding use or repayment. Reassigning a purpose or releasing a reservation shall create an audited allocation event with no financial posting.
LF06  Remaining allocation equals assigned received proceeds plus restored refunds, minus funded use, returned unused principal and released amounts, adjusted by allocation transfers. A reservation shall not be negative or exceed the positive cash balance of its holding account. An untagged spend that would breach this rule shall require reallocation or release before posting.
LF07  For a credit-card expense, the purpose tag shall create a funded commitment, not consume cash. The same amount shall remain reserved in a bank for later settlement and become unavailable to other allocations. Card payment shall consume that commitment and reservation once, without a second expense or a second purpose-spending count. Partial settlements shall explicitly identify commitment amounts.
Loan card and repayment distinction
The loan detail shall show received principal, outstanding principal, posted unpaid interest, lender, purpose, available purpose funding, card commitments, funded usage, released amounts and repayment history. Use “Remaining purpose funding,” never an unlabeled “available loan balance.”
Repayment from ordinary funds lowers debt and cash but does not erase prior funded use. If unused proceeds are returned, the repayment shall also explicitly reduce the reservation through a linked return event. Repaying principal shall never replenish an allocation automatically.

What each step means
Receipt adds INR 10,000 to bank cash and the same amount to debt. It is not salary or other income. Reserving it changes only its planning designation. After receipt, wallet available balance is INR 15,000 and unallocated cash is INR 5,000.
The INR 3,000 purchase debits Bank A only once. Its loan funding tag records INR 3,000 used for the startup. Loan principal remains INR 10,000 because paying a merchant does not pay the lender. Budget spending increases by INR 3,000.
The INR 2,200 repayment reduces cash by INR 2,200, debt by INR 2,000 and net worth by the INR 200 interest expense. Purpose funding stays INR 7,000. Unallocated cash becomes INR 2,800, which is sufficient to cover this payment without releasing the reservation.
Returning INR 1,000 of unused proceeds reduces both bank cash and principal by INR 1,000. The explicit return event reduces the reservation to INR 6,000. It creates no expense and leaves net worth unchanged.
Card purchase variant
Instead of the cash purchase, buy INR 3,000 of startup services on a card. Bank cash stays INR 15,000; card debt rises INR 3,000; budget expense and purpose usage rise INR 3,000. Of the INR 10,000 reservation, INR 3,000 is committed to card settlement and INR 7,000 remains available for new purpose spending. Paying the card later reduces bank cash and card debt by INR 3,000 and clears the commitment. It must not add another INR 3,000 expense.
The prohibited interpretation
Never subtract the purchase from the bank and again from a cash-like loan balance included in the wallet. Never reduce principal merely because proceeds were spent. Allocation movements are metadata entries linked to the financial event, not duplicate bank transactions.

AR01  All linked postings, loan or receivable changes, allocation events and balance-cache updates shall commit atomically. Account histories shall be projections of the same event; do not create independent editable duplicates.
AR02  Store integer minor units in BIGINT fields. INR 100.25 is 10025 paise. Parse decimal strings exactly; reject excess decimal places. Send large monetary values as decimal integer strings in JSON; do not use binary floating-point money arithmetic.
AR03  Posted journals shall be immutable. Reverse and replace to correct; prohibit isolated editing or deletion of one transfer leg. Preserve effective timestamp, server timestamp, actor, reason, idempotency key and reversal links.
AR04  Maintain deterministic ordering by effective timestamp and sequence ID. Backdated events and reversals shall rebuild affected running balances and period summaries. Display “balance after transaction” from that ordering, not a stale saved snapshot.
Interest agreements are informational until realized in V1. Interest paid or received is recognized at payment or receipt; no automatic receivable, payable or accrual is created merely by entering an agreed rate or amount.

8 Metrics budgets and spending insights
RP01  Available balance shall equal all recorded bank plus physical cash balances. Net worth shall equal bank and cash plus investment carrying values plus lent principal outstanding, minus credit card debt and borrowed principal outstanding. Any explicitly posted additional asset or liability adjustments shall be included once. Allocations and available credit limits shall never enter net worth.
RP02  Monthly wallet credits and debits shall mean movements across the liquid-wallet boundary: income, borrowing and returned receivables are inflows; purchases, investments, lending and debt payments are outflows. Transfers between own liquid accounts are excluded from both totals. Label these metrics “Liquid inflow” and “Liquid outflow,” and show income and actual expenses separately.
RP03  Account-level credits and debits shall include transfers affecting that account and provide an opening-to-closing reconciliation. For a card, use “Purchases and charges” and “Payments and refunds.” A card purchase is an expense immediately, but is not a liquid-wallet outflow until paid.
BG01  Seed five editable expense categories such as food, transport, shopping, bills and other. Support income and system event classifications separately so a transfer cannot become an expense merely by assigning a category. Split expenses may allocate amounts across categories, with splits summing exactly to the expense total.
BG02  For each calendar month, users shall set a nonnegative overall budget and allocate it among categories. Proposed V1 validation requires allocations to equal the overall budget before activation; a draft may be incomplete. No automatic carryover is assumed.
BG03  Budget actuals shall include posted expenses, including card purchases, paid loan interest and fees, less linked expense refunds. Exclude transfers, savings contributions, lending principal, borrowed proceeds, principal repayments and opening balances. Show actual, remaining and overrun for each category and the total.
BG04  Deleting a used category shall archive it or remap its transactions in a controlled operation, preserving history and report totals. Renaming shall preserve its ID. Categories omitted from an active budget shall appear as unbudgeted spending and still count in the overall expense total.
RP04  The monthly heatmap shall use the same expense basis as the budget. Each day shall show a value and accessible intensity legend; selecting it opens that day’s transactions. Zero spending is neutral. Negative net spending from refunds shall display explicitly with a separate indicator, not an invalid intensity.
RP05  Recommended heatmap bands are zero, up to 25%, up to 50%, up to 75% and above 75% of the highest positive daily expense in the selected month. Show that the scale is relative to the month. Date boundaries shall follow the wallet timezone, including midnight and month changes.
V1 refund policy: recognize a linked refund on its effective receipt date, including cross-month refunds. The original month is not silently restated. Restore the original funding usage only when the refund and payment medium permit it; a card refund first reduces its related commitment.

9 Data entities and relationships
Recommended relational model. All private entities carry wallet_id; private foreign keys must resolve within the same wallet. IDs are stable opaque identifiers. Amount fields use integer minor units and timestamps are stored in UTC with wallet timezone retained for reporting.

Assign, move, release, use, restore or return; allocation, transaction, amount and cash holding account.

Transaction component and allocation split; card-funded commitment links purchase, reservation and settlement or refund.
Category and Budget
Wallet categories and archive status; monthly budget total, status and category allocations with unique period keys.
AuditEvent and BalanceProjection
Actor, action, timestamps, references and change reason; rebuildable balances and period aggregates with ordering/version markers.

Relationships: a wallet owns many accounts and transactions; each transaction owns at least two journal lines. A loan has many disbursements, repayments and allocations. Each allocation has many events and may hold reservations in multiple cash accounts. Transaction components and allocations connect through funding usage records.

10 System architecture and integrity
Application structure
React with TypeScript and Tailwind CSS shall provide a responsive web interface. Organize screens around dashboard, accounts, transactions, budgets, investments and lending and borrowing. Share validated form schemas where practical, but keep the backend authoritative. Exact component libraries and versions remain implementation choices.
Node.js with TypeScript shall expose an authenticated API. Recommended modules are identity, institutions, account management, journal posting, loans and receivables, allocations, reporting and budgets. Use a relational database such as PostgreSQL for transactions, constraints and concurrency control; store validated logos in object storage.
Request flow: React form → authenticated API → event validation → accounting and allocation service → atomic database transaction → committed event and refreshed projections → UI. Domain services shall own money rules; controllers and UI components must not implement separate accounting formulas.
API capability contract
Capability
Contract
Accounts and catalog
Create, list, update and archive accounts; search or submit institutions; set validated openings.
Post event
Accept event type, account IDs, exact amounts, timestamp, categories, component splits and funding assignments with idempotency key.
Correct event
Reverse and optionally replace a complete posted event with reason and expected version.
Funding operations
Assign, move and release reservations; link usage and commitments. Validate funding and cash capacity server-side.
Read models
Return dashboard, chronological history, loan detail, monthly budget and heatmap from a consistent cutoff and timezone.

DB01  Enforce balanced journals at commit, positive line amounts, supported event/account combinations, unique idempotency keys per wallet, valid ownership and no orphaned linked records. Incoming duplicate keys with different payloads shall return a conflict.
DB02  Serialize conflicting account and allocation mutations using row locks or equivalent concurrency control in a stable lock order. Revalidate balances and funding capacity inside the transaction; retries shall remain idempotent.
DB03  Budget allocations, component splits and funding splits shall satisfy their sum constraints. Liability principal cannot be repaid below zero in V1. Archive operations shall preserve foreign keys. Index wallet and effective date, account and sequence, category and period, loan and transaction references.
DB04  Journal lines shall be the source of truth. Cached balances and report summaries shall be rebuildable and reconciled against journals. Posting shall not depend on a delayed report job to enforce available funds or reservation constraints.

11 Security and operational requirements
NF01  Every private read and write shall authorize wallet ownership on the server, including indirect references, exported data and uploaded objects. Tests shall verify that changing an object ID cannot expose another user’s data. Shared institution endpoints shall return catalog data only.
NF02  Use authenticated sessions with protected cookies or an equivalent secure token design. Protect credentials and secrets, use encrypted transport and encrypted storage, rate-limit authentication and mutation endpoints, and avoid storing payment credentials, PINs or full card numbers.
NF03  Maintain an audit trail for financial corrections, openings, catalog changes and funding releases. Application logs shall not include authentication secrets or unrestricted financial payloads. Operational staff access shall be restricted and auditable.
NF04  Recommended release target: ordinary reads and transaction saves complete within two seconds at the 95th percentile under a defined test workload of 50 concurrent users and 100,000 transactions per tested wallet. Measure on the staging configuration; tune indexes and paginate history before release.
NF05  Provide loading, empty, validation and recoverable-error states. Core forms, account cards and heatmap drilldowns shall work with keyboard navigation, visible focus, labels and screen-reader text. Information shall not depend on color alone.
NF06  Financial posting shall remain all-or-nothing during server failures. A client retry after an unknown response shall retrieve the original result using its idempotency key. Offline writes are outside V1; show that a save is pending or failed rather than presenting it as posted.
NF07  Recommended operations baseline: daily encrypted backups, recovery point within 24 hours and recovery time within four hours. A restore drill shall reconstruct journal totals, allocations and balances before release. Final production service targets shall be approved during deployment planning.
NF08  Store user timestamps with explicit timezone handling and retain effective versus recorded times. Money shall display using currency formatting with two decimals for INR, while retaining exact integer values throughout all calculations.
Reconciliation and observability
Monitor failed postings, duplicate request conflicts, journal imbalance attempts, projection drift and reservation violations without logging sensitive content. Provide a reconciliation report comparing journal-derived balances with stored projections. A balance correction must create an explicit adjustment with a reason; it must never silently overwrite history or be classified as salary.
Data portability and retention
Recommended V1 support includes exporting a user’s account and transaction history in a machine-readable format. Account closure and data deletion policy must distinguish archiving from permanent deletion and handle backups consistently. Retention periods and production authentication provider are deployment decisions, not confirmed financial scope.

12 Edge cases and expected behavior
Case
Required handling
Insufficient cash or card capacity
Reject ordinary overspending in V1; show available amount. A permitted overdraft requires a separately designed policy, not a hidden negative balance.
Loan repayment exceeds principal
Reject excess principal. Interest and fees must be entered separately. Do not create negative debt or treat the excess as income.
Existing loan at onboarding
Create opening liability only. If remaining borrowed cash is already in a bank opening balance, attach an opening funding reservation without crediting cash again.
Several disbursements or lenders
Append draws to the correct loan; each draw links its bank receipt. Different legal debts remain distinct records even if purposes match.
Loan paid but purpose funds remain
Require release or reassignment of remaining reservation before closure. Do not delete historical funding attribution.
Reserved funds moved or spent
Move reservation with bank transfer; for unrelated use, require explicit release or reassignment. Block historical edits that make reservations infeasible.
Refund after partial card payment
Restore only unsettled commitment first; any remaining refund reduces card debt. Do not create bank cash until actually received. Reject card credit balances in V1 or record their explicit conversion to a receivable through a supported adjustment.
Backdated edit crosses months
Reverse and replace; recompute affected account histories, budgets, heatmaps and funding chronology. Reject if it would create a historically invalid balance or allocation.
Lent principal not recovered
Keep overdue receivable until explicit write-off. A write-off reduces the asset and net worth as a separately labeled noncash loss; it is excluded from the cash-spending budget.
Investment withdrawal or gain
Ordinary expense form cannot spend an investment. A dedicated return-of-capital event may reduce recorded contribution value and credit bank cash; gains and market valuation are deferred.
Loan fee withheld at receipt
Record gross principal as liability, net amount into bank and withheld fee as expense. Allocate only net proceeds actually received.
Duplicate institution or transaction
Apply normalized catalog uniqueness and request idempotency. Similar legitimate repeated purchases remain allowed with distinct user intents.
Zero budget or unused category
Show actual amounts and overrun without division by zero. Archived categories remain visible in historical periods.
Future dated record or scheduled SIP
V1 posts only events effective now or earlier. A future plan must not alter balances; scheduling is deferred.

Delivery sequencing
Build the posting engine before dashboard calculations so every later feature uses the same accounting basis. Complete loan funding and repayment behavior before treating loan cards as finished. Add the heatmap and budgets only after event classification and refund rules are stable.
Definition of done
A feature is complete when its requirements are implemented, permission checks and validations run on the server, corresponding acceptance criteria pass, linked events reconcile, user-facing labels distinguish cash from debt and allocations, and the workflow works on mobile and desktop. Document migrations and provide a recovery procedure for any financial data transformation.
No fixed calendar or staffing estimate is assumed. Estimate each phase after architecture setup and a small end-to-end posting prototype. The product owner reviews scope and interface terminology; engineering owns integrity and recovery verification; testers verify the acceptance matrix.
